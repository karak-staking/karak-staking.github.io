Karak Staking.

Karak Staking is a form of universal restaking: instead of letting your ETH or liquid staking tokens sit idle after they finish securing Ethereum consensus, you commit them again through Karak's K2 layer to back Distributed Secure Services — oracles, bridges, data-availability networks and other infrastructure that needs its own pool of economic security. The same capital that already earns a base staking yield takes on a second job, and in return you earn an additional reward stream from every DSS it helps secure. That second job carries its own slashing conditions, separate from the ones your validator already answers to, which is the trade-off every Karak Staking participant is accepting.

What Is Karak Staking?

Karak Staking is the act of restaking already-staked ETH, liquid staking tokens or other supported assets into Karak's K2 layer so that same capital can back Distributed Secure Services and earn an additional reward on top of its base yield. Rather than building one more isolated staking pool, Karak treats restaking as a universal layer: any asset with a verifiable security guarantee — a validator's bond, an LST's underlying stake, even certain stablecoins — can be deposited once and then allocated across many services that each need their own economic backstop. A deposit does not leave your control to a centralized custodian; it is held in a smart contract and allocated according to the DSS modules you opt into.

Restaking in one sentence

Restaking reuses capital that is already providing one security guarantee to provide a second, unrelated one, a concept popularized by shared-security protocols and explained in general terms in Karak's own documentation. The appeal is capital efficiency: your ETH does not need to be split or duplicated to do more than one job.

Distributed Secure Services, briefly

A DSS is any off-chain or cross-chain system — an oracle network, a bridge validator set, a data-availability committee — that needs economic skin in the game to deter misbehavior but does not want to bootstrap its own token and validator set from zero. Karak Staking lets such a service rent security from restakers instead, an approach similar in spirit to the restaking model EigenLayer popularized on Ethereum.

Why assets beyond ETH matter

Because K2 is built to be asset-agnostic, Karak Staking is not limited to native ETH. Liquid staking tokens, wrapped BTC positions and other yield-bearing collateral can be restaked, which widens the pool of capital available to secure DSS modules without forcing every participant to unwind an existing staking position first.

How Karak Staking Secures Distributed Secure Services

When you commit assets through Karak Staking, the K2 layer does not simply hold them — it assigns them, according to your choices, as backing collateral for one or more DSS modules. Each module defines its own slashing rules, reward rate and operational requirements, and operators who run the actual infrastructure for that service draw on the restaked collateral as their bond. If an operator acts correctly, restakers earn their share of the service's rewards; if an operator misbehaves in a way the module can prove, a portion of the restaked collateral backing that operator is slashed.

Karak Staking Rewards and the K2 Layer

The reward a Karak Staking position earns has two layers stacked on top of each other. The first is whatever your underlying asset already pays — native ETH staking yield, or the yield embedded in an LST you hold. The second is the DSS reward Karak distributes for the security you are providing through K2, paid in the terms each individual module sets, often a mix of its own token and a share of the fees it collects. Calculating Your Karak Staking Rewards

A rewards calculator for Karak Staking only needs three inputs: how much you plan to restake, the combined APY you expect to earn across the base yield and the DSS reward layer, and the period you want to project over. Multiply the amount by the APY, then scale that figure by the fraction of a year the period represents, and you get an estimate of what the position should return before any slashing event. The flow below is how most people arrive at that number before committing funds.
  • Enter the amount of ETH or LST you intend to restake through Karak.
  • Enter the expected combined APY — base staking yield plus the DSS reward rate for the modules you plan to back.
  • Choose a period: days, months or a full year.
  • Review the projected reward alongside the slashing conditions of each DSS module before depositing.
  • Confirm the restaking transaction and track accrued rewards from your dashboard.

Karak Staking Risks: Slashing and DSS Exposure

The extra yield Karak Staking offers is compensation for an extra risk, not a free bonus. Understanding exactly what can be slashed, and under what conditions, is the difference between an informed restaker and someone who finds out the hard way.

Slashing beyond consensus rules

Your validator can already be slashed for breaking Ethereum consensus rules, and ethereum.org's staking overview covers that base risk in detail. Karak Staking adds a second, independent slashing surface: each DSS module defines its own conditions for provable misbehavior, and a slash under those conditions touches the restaked collateral backing that module regardless of whether your validator ever did anything wrong at the consensus layer.

Why diversification across DSS matters

Backing a single DSS module concentrates your added risk in that module's specific failure modes. Spreading restaked collateral across several independently audited services, and checking each one's track record before opting in, keeps a problem in one module from draining a position that was meant to earn a broadly diversified reward.

Karak Staking vs Traditional ETH Staking

Traditional ETH staking asks for one thing — run or delegate to a validator that follows consensus rules — and pays one yield in return. Karak Staking starts from that same staked or liquid position and adds a second, optional layer of risk and reward on top of it, without requiring you to withdraw from your existing stake first.

What stays the same

Your base ETH stake keeps earning its ordinary consensus reward the whole time, and any transaction tied to your position can still be verified independently on Etherscan. Karak Staking does not replace that yield; it sits alongside it.

What changes

What changes is exposure. A plain validator answers only to Ethereum's own slashing rules, a system described on the Ethereum project page. A restaked position through Karak also answers to every DSS module's own rules, which is a wider attack surface in exchange for a wider reward surface.

Getting Started with Karak Staking

Starting with Karak Staking means connecting a self-custody wallet, depositing ETH or a supported LST into K2, and then actively choosing which Distributed Secure Services that collateral will back — Karak does not assign it for you. Read each module's slashing terms before opting in, keep the combined APY estimate realistic by accounting for the slashing probability you are comfortable with, and treat the base staking yield and the DSS reward as two separate lines you can track and withdraw according to each module's own rules.
What is Karak Staking in simple terms?
Karak Staking is restaking ETH, LSTs or other supported assets through Karak's K2 layer so that collateral also secures Distributed Secure Services, earning an additional reward on top of whatever base staking yield it already produces.
What is a Distributed Secure Service (DSS)?
A DSS is infrastructure — an oracle, a bridge, a data-availability network — that needs economic security but does not want to bootstrap its own validator set. Karak Staking lets restakers supply that security in exchange for a share of the service's rewards.
Can I restake assets other than ETH on Karak?
Yes. K2 is designed to be asset-agnostic, so liquid staking tokens and other yield-bearing collateral can be restaked alongside native ETH, widening the pool available to back DSS modules.
How is a Karak Staking reward calculated?
Take the amount restaked, multiply it by the combined APY — base staking yield plus the DSS reward rate — and scale that by the fraction of the year you are projecting over. That gives an estimate before accounting for any slashing event.
Is Karak Staking riskier than regular ETH staking?
It carries an additional risk layer. Regular ETH staking is only exposed to Ethereum's own slashing rules, while Karak Staking adds each DSS module's own slashing conditions on top of that, in exchange for an extra reward.
Do I lose my base staking yield when I restake through Karak?
No. The base yield from your ETH or LST keeps accruing as usual; the DSS reward from Karak Staking is a second, separate layer added on top rather than a replacement.
How do I reduce slashing risk with Karak Staking?
Diversify restaked collateral across multiple independently reviewed DSS modules rather than concentrating it in one, and read each module's specific slashing conditions before opting in.
